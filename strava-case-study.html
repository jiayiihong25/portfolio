Strava: The Pre-Activity Social Tool

Gamifying the pre-activity experience for intermittent Strava users to overcome the mental activation barrier and avoid skipping planned workouts.

Role Product Designer
Timeline 3 Weeks (2025–2026)
Context Self-Directed Product Strategy
Deliverables PRD, Interactive Prototype, User Flow
⚡ Key Outcome
Designed a 4-part social accountability loop embedded in the Record tab, validating a one-tap start flow tested with 8 lapsed users that reduced pre-activity dropoff and accelerated workout initiation.
01 Context

Background & Opportunity

The energy required to transition from a resting state to a workout-ready state represents a significant friction point for Strava’s largest user segment: the intermittent user. Unlike running fanatics who are intrinsically motivated by established rigid habit loops, intermittent users possess the intent to exercise (evidenced by downloading Strava), but frequently fail at the point of execution due to immediate distractions or low activation energy.

Currently, Strava caters primarily to the post-activity flex, leaving the intermittent user alone during the most difficult part of the journey: the critical 15-minute preparation window where they are most likely to abandon their intent entirely.

02 Problem

The Core Friction

1. Barrier to Retention

When the app only supports the finish line, users associate Strava with the pressure of being a perfect athlete. For intermittent users, the app becomes a negative reminder of failed plans, causing eventual churn.

2. Mission Alignment Gap

Strava aims to connect athletes to what motivates them, but offers zero support during the hardest 15 minutes of workout prep.

3. Losing the Battle for Attention

In the 15 minutes before a workout, Strava is in direct competition with social media and passive phone entertainment. Remaining silent lets low-effort distractions win.

03 Process

Research & Insights

We conducted user interviews with intermittent athletes aged 18–34 to identify why planned workouts fell through:

• Motivation & Mental Hurdle: Skipping was decided well before the workout window, driven by passive phone use. Knowing a specific friend was actively getting ready or waiting was the one consistent override.

• Physical Preparation Threshold: Once participants began physically lacing up their shoes, they followed through. The decision to skip forms before any physical action occurs.

04 Solution

The 4-Part Social Accountability Loop

1. Lacing Up Notification

When a friend toggles "Lacing Up," connections receive a high-priority trigger inviting them off whatever they're doing and into Strava.

2. "Waiting on You" Escalation Ping

If unconfirmed within 5 minutes, an ambient notification activates social peer accountability.

3. Lacing Up Full-Screen Commitment

One-tap "I'm Getting Ready!" action with a live Group Status bar showing who is preparing.

4. Live Ready Room Module

Persistent real-time module on the Record tab displaying elapsed friend readiness to push the final step to pressing START.

05 Outcome

Validation & Rollout Strategy

A multi-stage rollout framework targeted a 10% increase in weekly workout consistency among intermittent segments while protecting core activity tracking workflows.
